Motherhood in Crisis Reflection

The graphic novel “Motherhood in Crisis” by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah highlights the harsh realities that women in Sierra Leone face during pregnancy and childbirth. What was most shocking for me to realize was just how deadly a natural process such as giving birth can be in countries where access to healthcare is poor. According to the article, one in ten women dies in childbirth in Sierra Leone.

Each woman’s story made the crisis seem more personal and relevant than just numbers and statistics could. For instance, in Memunatu’s story, one can see how geographical remoteness and lack of access to proper care make giving birth potentially lethal for women. In Heela’s case, one can observe how educational prospects may get compromised due to early pregnancy. Kadiatu’s story speaks of the emotional component and terror experienced by the women. Finally, Tetteh’s story brings up the connection between motherhood and imprisonment. Overall, these stories demonstrate that motherhood is an issue beyond healthcare.

Something that I thought was particularly impactful was the graphic novel style employed throughout the article. Instead of simply reading the paragraphs, the images aided my understanding of the emotional experience of each of the women. T he color choices, expressions, and concise writing all brought their story alive and made it more relatable. In addition, it made certain subjects, such as mortality, more palatable without detracting from their gravity. I believe that this style was particularly effective in being able to reach a broader audience.

The article also demonstrates how bigger problems such as epidemics and the lack of effective health services exacerbate the problem further. As seen during the Ebola and coronavirus epidemics, hospitals have become too overloaded to help pregnant women. This is related to what has been discussed in relation to global health problems and their impacts on vulnerable groups. The women of Sierra Leone not only suffer from pregnancy but also a lack of systems to help them.

The other significant theme that comes out of these stories is the lack of recognition of women’s voices. By writing their stories, the writers give them a voice that makes the world see their challenges. This taught me the significance of storytelling in addressing issues. If not for these stories, no one would know the extent of problems regarding maternal health in these regions.

In conclusion, this piece of writing affected my view on motherhood. While in many societies, giving birth to children is considered a happy moment in their lives, in Sierra Leone, childbirth may be dangerous and even deadly. The graphic novel illustrates that the enhancement of maternal wellbeing does not only include implementing changes and visiting Hospitals. Rather, it also implies providing women with better education and community support.
